## 1. Purpose

This document identifies potential research gaps relevant to ExoAnalytica.

The project aims to investigate an AI-assisted, multi-method exoplanet analysis framework using:

1. Transit photometry
2. Radial velocity (RV)
3. Gravitational microlensing

The objective is not simply to combine three datasets or detection methods. The research should investigate how physically meaningful evidence, measurement uncertainty, observational limitations, and method-specific results can be represented and interpreted within a coherent analysis framework.

The gaps identified here are preliminary hypotheses. They must be tested through a systematic literature review and experimental investigation before being claimed as novel contributions.

## 2. Existing Research Landscape

### 2.1 Transit-based machine learning

ExoMiner and related approaches demonstrate the use of deep learning for transit candidate validation.

PlanetNet-MMG (2026) presents a multimodal classification architecture integrating:

- Structured stellar metadata
- Light-curve temporal information
- Graph-based relationships between astronomical objects

This work demonstrates that multimodal learning and relational reasoning are already being investigated in exoplanet classification.

Therefore, ExoAnalytica must not claim that multimodal exoplanet classification itself is a new contribution.

### 2.2 Transit and radial velocity joint analysis

The juliet framework supports joint modelling of transit photometry and radial velocity observations, including model comparison and stochastic processes.

Consequently, combining transit and RV observations alone is not sufficient to establish research novelty.

RV analysis also presents important challenges:

- Stellar activity
- Instrumental noise
- Temporally correlated signals
- Uneven observation sampling
- Model-selection uncertainty

These limitations are discussed by Hara and Ford (2023).

### 2.3 Gravitational microlensing

Microlensing provides a complementary detection method, particularly for planetary systems and orbital configurations that are difficult to access using conventional transit and RV surveys.

However, microlensing observations have distinct characteristics:

- Short-lived magnification events
- Planetary perturbations in lensing light curves
- Degenerate physical solutions
- Uncertainty in lens and host characterization
- Different selection effects from transit and RV surveys

Microlensing should therefore be treated as a physically distinct analysis modality rather than another ordinary time-series input.

Roman's Galactic Bulge Time-Domain Survey (GBTDS) is designed as a dedicated space-based microlensing survey. Its planned observing strategy includes high-cadence photometry and additional lower-cadence observations. The RMDC26 challenge provides semi-realistic simulated Roman-like photometric and astrometric event data.

This creates a practical opportunity to investigate microlensing analysis before public GBTDS survey observations become available. However, performance on RMDC26 simulations must not be presented as validation on real Roman observations. The simulated Experienced tier also has a deliberately selected event sample and should not be treated as a representative survey population without accounting for its selection.



### 2.4 Existing data infrastructure

The NASA Exoplanet Archive provides access to multiple categories of astronomical information, including:

- Planetary parameters
- Stellar parameters
- Photometric observations
- Published radial velocity data
- Microlensing-related catalogues and resources

However, the availability of different data products does not automatically establish that observations can be cross-matched to the same planetary system.

Cross-identification must be scientifically verified.

## 3. Preliminary Research Gap Hypotheses

### G1. Cross-method evidence integration

**Problem:**

Transit, RV, and microlensing observations are generated by different physical processes and have different observational constraints.

A common framework must preserve the physical meaning of each method instead of treating all measurements as interchangeable model features.

**Research opportunity:**

Investigate a framework containing separate method-specific analysis modules and a common evidence-integration layer.

Potential components:

- Transit analysis module
- RV analysis module
- Microlensing analysis module
- Candidate and host identification layer
- Evidence and uncertainty integration layer

**Research question:**

Can physically distinct detection-method outputs be represented and compared within a common evidence framework without losing their method-specific assumptions and limitations?

### G2. Missing-modality analysis

**Problem:**

A planetary candidate may have transit data without RV follow-up, RV measurements without a detected transit, or microlensing observations without equivalent photometric or spectroscopic follow-up.

Requiring all modalities may exclude scientifically useful candidates.

**Research opportunity:**

Investigate methods that can analyse available evidence while explicitly identifying missing observations.

The framework should distinguish:

- Evidence available
- Evidence unavailable
- Evidence not applicable
- Evidence that has not yet been collected

Missing data must not be interpreted as negative evidence.

**Research question:**

How can an exoplanet analysis framework maintain scientifically meaningful results when one or more observational modalities are unavailable?

### G3. Detection bias and population representation

**Problem:**

Different detection techniques are sensitive to different planetary populations.

Transit surveys favour geometries in which planets cross the stellar disc from the observer's perspective.

RV sensitivity depends on planetary mass, orbital properties, stellar activity, and measurement precision.

Microlensing probes different regions of planetary parameter space and has its own selection effects.

**Research opportunity:**

Investigate how method-specific detection biases affect comparisons between candidate populations.

Potential considerations:

- Detection efficiency
- Survey selection functions
- Observational completeness
- False-positive rates
- Population-level uncertainty

**Research question:**

How can method-specific detection biases be represented when combining or comparing results from multiple exoplanet detection techniques?

### G4. Uncertainty-aware characterization

**Problem:**

A model classification score is not equivalent to a scientifically calibrated probability that a planet exists.

Similarly, a point estimate of planetary mass or radius does not communicate the complete measurement uncertainty.

**Research opportunity:**

Develop an analysis strategy that distinguishes:

- Classification confidence
- Measurement uncertainty
- Parameter posterior distributions
- Model uncertainty
- Detection significance
- Independent observational evidence

Potential planetary parameters include:

- Planet radius
- Planet mass
- Orbital period
- Orbital eccentricity
- Planet density
- Host-star parameters

The framework must not report parameters that cannot be supported by the available observations.

**Research question:**

How can uncertainty from different detection methods be propagated into a consistent planetary characterization result?

### G5. Candidate identity and cross-method association

**Problem:**

Different observations do not necessarily refer to the same planetary object.

A star identifier, a microlensing event identifier, a survey target identifier, and a planetary system identifier are not interchangeable.

Incorrect associations could produce scientifically invalid conclusions.

**Research opportunity:**

Investigate a provenance-aware identification layer that records:

- Target identifiers
- Host-star identifiers
- Planet identifiers
- Microlensing event identifiers
- Observation sources
- Cross-match confidence
- Literature references

The framework should distinguish confirmed associations from possible associations.

**Research question:**

How can observations from different detection methods be associated without introducing unsupported assumptions about planetary identity?



### G6. Simulation-to-observation transfer and astrometric microlensing evidence

**Problem:**

Roman-like simulated microlensing datasets provide an opportunity to develop and test methods before suitable public survey observations are available. However, simulated data cannot fully represent all properties of real observations, including calibration effects, crowding, instrumental behaviour, and unmodelled astrophysical systematics.

Photometric and astrometric microlensing measurements also constrain lens systems in different ways. Combining them requires an appropriate physical model and careful distinction between the background source and foreground lens/host.

**Research opportunity:**

Investigate:
- Event and parameter recovery using RMDC26 simulated data.
- Joint photometric and astrometric modelling where both data types are supplied.
- Sensitivity to event complexity, cadence, noise, and missing measurements.
- Simulation-to-observation domain shift and the limits of simulation-based validation.
- A reproducible transition from simulation benchmarks to future public Roman observations.

**Research question:**

How reliably can microlensing models recover event and planetary parameters from Roman-like simulated photometric and astrometric data, and what additional validation is required before transferring those methods to real Roman observations?

This is a candidate research gap for investigation, not an established novel contribution.

## 5. Proposed Research Direction

The current proposed direction is:

**An uncertainty-aware, multi-method exoplanet analysis framework integrating physically grounded evidence from transit photometry, radial velocity measurements, and gravitational microlensing.**

The framework should investigate:

1. Method-specific physical analysis.
2. A common representation of scientific evidence.
3. Explicit handling of missing observational modalities.
4. Uncertainty-aware planetary characterization.
5. Detection-bias awareness.
6. Reliable cross-identification and data provenance.
7. Transparent reporting of evidence and limitations.\n8. Simulation-to-observation validation for Roman-like microlensing data, including photometric and astrometric evidence where available.

The proposed framework should not assume that all three methods observe the same planet.

Two distinct analysis scenarios must be supported:

**Scenario A - Same-system evidence integration**

Multiple methods provide observations associated with the same planetary system, and the association can be scientifically justified.

**Scenario B - Independent candidate analysis**

Candidates detected by different methods are analysed separately and compared at the population or catalogue level without assuming they represent the same object.

## 6. Preliminary Research Questions

**RQ1:** How can transit, RV, and microlensing analyses be integrated while preserving the physical assumptions of each detection method?

**RQ2:** How can uncertainty be propagated across method-specific analysis modules?

**RQ3:** How can the framework handle missing observational modalities without treating missing evidence as negative evidence?

**RQ4:** How can cross-method associations be established and documented reliably?

**RQ5:** How do method-specific selection effects influence the interpretation of combined exoplanet candidate populations?\n\n**RQ6:** How reliably can microlensing parameters be recovered from Roman-like simulated photometric and astrometric data, and how should simulation-to-observation limitations be evaluated?

## 8. Scientific Risks and Limitations

### 8.1 False novelty claims

Existing research already covers multimodal classification, transit-RV joint modelling, and machine-learning applications to microlensing.

The proposed contribution must be distinguished from these existing works.

### 8.2 Incompatible data products

Different surveys use different identifiers, measurement formats, time systems, uncertainty conventions, and selection criteria.

A common data representation must not erase important scientific information.

### 8.3 Microlensing limitations

Microlensing event parameters do not always uniquely determine the physical properties of the lens system.

The framework must preserve alternative solutions and relevant degeneracies.

### 8.4 Insufficient cross-method overlap

There may be limited public data where the same planetary system has useful observations from all three detection techniques.

A three-method architecture must not depend on an unrealistic assumption of complete observational overlap.

### 8.5 Simulation-to-observation limitations\n\nRMDC26 is a semi-realistic simulation resource, not a substitute for real Roman survey observations. The Experienced tier is filtered for likely anomalous events and is not representative of the full expected event population. Any performance result must identify the dataset tier, selection process, simulated observing assumptions, and evaluation split. Real-observation validation remains a separate future requirement.\n\n### 8.6 Model validation

High classification accuracy alone is insufficient.

Validation should include suitable baselines, independent test samples, uncertainty calibration, selection-bias analysis, and scientifically justified parameter comparisons.

## 9. Preliminary Gap Statement

The current research-gap hypothesis is:

"An uncertainty-aware, multi-method exoplanet analysis framework that integrates physically grounded evidence from transit photometry, radial velocity measurements, and gravitational microlensing, while explicitly accounting for heterogeneous observations, missing modalities, detection biases, parameter uncertainty, and cross-method identification uncertainty."

This statement is a proposed research direction, not yet a demonstrated novelty claim.

## 10. Required Next Investigations

Before finalizing the research gap, the following investigations are required:

- Systematic review of multimodal exoplanet classification.
- Detailed assessment of PlanetNet-MMG (2026).
- Detailed assessment of juliet and other joint transit-RV frameworks.
- Review of machine-learning-based microlensing analysis.
- Review of Bayesian evidence integration and uncertainty propagation.
- Investigation of cross-matching and selection functions.
- Dataset availability and compatibility assessment.
